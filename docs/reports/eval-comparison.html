“Cannot fail” is the category that matters. A confusion matrix built with FN = 0 pins recall at 1.0000 and makes accuracy identical to precision, so four reported metrics carry one measurement. Against a threshold, that indicator can never trip — the gate is decorative. This is not a criticism of the models; it is a statement about what the published numbers are able to tell anyone.

4Is the comparison fair?

Verdict: not-comparable. COMPLETENESS: all five required areas were received - documents, sentiment, sentiment_mnp, sentiment_retention, sentiment_telesale - each with a non-empty survey AND a non-empty adversarial verdict. Nothing was missing and nothing was substituted. Every area's verdict refuted more than two claims (6, 5, 7, 6 and 6 respectively), so all five areas are recorded at LOW confidence per the rule, and every refuted claim has been dropped or restated. VERDICT: as a model-versus-model comparison this evidence base is not comparable, and the reason is not the scorer. Scoring is the strongest part: one shared maths module (src/google_model/metrics.py) computes every count, average, CER and cosine for both sides, and the two arms' scorer wrappers, although forked, contain no differing metric logic today. The problem is everything upstream of it. The two sides are fed different artefacts by construction (raw audio versus an ASR-plus-LLM-rewritten transcript in four areas; a PDF text layer versus a 200-DPI raster in the fifth), run different prompts (documents v1 vs v2, telesale one 167 KB monolith vs four trimmed prompts, mnp/retention a wide chain-of-thought schema vs a narrow label-only one), decode under different samplers and output ceilings, get different retry and resume budgets, and read from two physically separate corpus copies that nothing verifies and whose counts are known to differ in at least three areas.
