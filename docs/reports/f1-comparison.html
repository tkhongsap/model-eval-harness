Can Gemini 2.5 Flash be replaced by models on our own GPU?

Weighted F1 across four models on 188 hand-labelled calls — and, more importantly, which of those differences survive a statistical test.

The short answer

Qwen3.8 27B Viable No detectable difference from Gemini on labelling
Qwen3.6 27B Viable Also indistinguishable, marginally weaker
Gemma 4 12B Excluded Measurably behind on all three dimensions
Qwen3-ASR 1.7B Blocked 2.6× the character error rate, plus a failure mode

The labelling stage and the transcription stage give different answers, which is why the recommendation splits them: move text → label onto the internal GPU, keep audio → text external.

The lead changes sides between packs.

On call_result, Qwen3.8 is ahead by 1.1 on pack A and behind by 2.1 on pack B. A quantity that changes sign between two samples of the same population is noise, not a ranking — which is exactly what the paired test concludes independently.

Is any of it a real difference?

F1 alone will mislead you here. The test that decides is a paired sign test at α = 1/64, and the number that matters is d — the count of calls where exactly one model was right. Calls both models get right, or both get wrong, carry no information about which is better.

“Underpowered” is not “the same”.

On call_result the two models disagreed on four calls out of 188 — below the six the test needs at this α. That is we cannot tell, not we have shown they match, and the harness refuses to collapse the two. The one dimension with enough disagreement to test, reason, comes back genuinely indistinguishable: an observed gap of 6 against a noise band of 16.

Why so little disagreement? Not because the task is easy — because the errors are shared. Each arm gets 13 call_result calls wrong and 11 are the same calls; co-failure runs about 12× what independence would predict. Both models fail the same hard calls, which is a more useful finding than a tie.

Same count, different calls: the pooled 2×2 is both-right 173, both-wrong 11, Gemini-only 2, Qwen-only 2. They disagree on four calls, two each way, and those cancel exactly on pooling. The interval is roughly ±4 points — wider than every per-dimension difference in this report.

What the F1 column hides

Every model has excellent recall and mediocre precision on reason. F1 averages the two and conceals it.

Every model over-predicts reasons — it lists reasons the call does not support. That is a property of the task and the prompt, not of any candidate, and the incumbent has it too. It is the largest quality gap in the labelling stage and no weighted average could show it. Gemma’s 71.3 is the only figure anywhere below production’s own floor, which is what excludes it — on True’s published criteria, not ours.

The transcription stage is a different story

Gemini — character error 0.044 Normalised CER, 20-call set
Qwen3-ASR — character error 0.115 2.6× worse · BEHIND at α 1/64
Runaway rate, 138 calls 13.9% 19 of 137  [8.7, 21.4]
New today, and it moves the wrong way.

The 20-call set showed one repetition runaway in twenty — 5%. Transcribing all 138 new calls produced 19 in 137, or 13.9%. The earlier estimate was not wrong so much as uninformative: its interval spanned 0.8–26.2% and contained the new figure all along. A twenty-call sample could never have bounded this.

One further call, ASR-027, failed transcription outright after six attempts. It is the second-shortest call in the set, so length does not explain it.

Character error rate is reported here as a diagnostic — it explains why the arms differ, and the preregistration explicitly forbids it from carrying the decision. What blocks the ASR migration is not the average; it is a catastrophic failure on roughly one call in seven, which no chunking configuration has removed.

End to end: audio in, decision out

Everything above measures the two stages separately. This measures the whole pipeline against business labels authored before any dialogue was written — the thing the previous experiment could not do, because it could only check whether two arms agreed, and two pipelines wrong in the same way agree perfectly.

The internal pipeline produces better business decisions than production — despite transcribing worse.

Qwen3-ASR has 2.6× the character error rate and loses roughly one call in eight to repetition runaways. The pipeline built on it still wins the save/churn decision. A stronger reasoner reading a flawed transcript beats a weaker one reading the audio.

Where Gemini loses: it misses churn

Product-matched calls only, so the outcome is compared like for like.
Arm outcome correct churn recall save recall
Gemini audio 56/92  61% 33/52  63% 23/24  96%
Qwen pipeline 74/103  72% 43/55  78% 31/31  100%

Gemini records 19 of 52 churning customers as saved. For a Retention pipeline that is the expensive direction to be wrong in — a missed churn is a customer who leaves without anyone knowing they were at risk. Qwen misses 12 of 55.

Read these as upper bounds, not as production numbers.

This corpus states its labels in every call; real calls rarely do, so both arms will score lower in production. And no arm exceeds ~0.66 because neither labeller ever emits unknown or undefined — 21 of 136 calls are unwinnable for everyone, which caps the whole table.

Gemini's 20 parse failures did not cost it the comparison: no call lost all three replicates, so every item stayed scoreable. Its deficit is genuine labelling, not truncated JSON. But production has no second replicate to fall back on.
