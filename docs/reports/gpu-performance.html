The real-data run is the decision-grade one, and it does not favour us. Its ground truth is a human-maintained sheet rather than generated, its audio is real customer calls from GCS, and it runs the incumbent on Vertex AI batch — how production actually runs it. Ours does none of those three.

Why the reversal is plausible rather than a contradiction to explain away. Our corpus states every label aloud — a documented property, not a discovery. That removes the need for the inferential step, and inference is exactly what a direct-audio model may be better at, since a transcript throws away prosody, hesitation and tone. So the synthetic set may not merely inflate scores; it may remove the thing the incumbent is good at. That mechanism is a hypothesis. The reversal is a measurement.

Do not quote the synthetic numbers as a production result. They remain useful for what they were built for — comparing pipeline shapes under controlled conditions — and they are not evidence about production.

Typhoon is the clear choice, and not on error rate alone. 2.5× better character error rate, and more importantly zero catastrophic runaways against 16 — a runaway is a transcript that repeats a fragment hundreds of times and is unusable, not merely wrong. Qwen also lost 16 calls from the scoreable set entirely.

Qwen3-ASR has been unavailable since 21 August — 10 h 40 min with no successful response on the day, and not re-run since. It stopped at 120 of 138 transcripts, below our 90% coverage gate, so it is reported unavailable rather than scored on what survived. The labeller sharing the same gateway answered throughout, so this is that model rather than the platform. One caveat in Qwen's favour: its entity accuracy is marginally higher (96.0% vs 94.6%), so it is not worse at everything.

4What this says, and what it does not

On synthetic data the internal pipeline beats production on both dimensions that matter, at no metered cost and with cleaner JSON (414/414 against 411/414). On real data it does not — it loses all three dimensions narrowly, and loses latency heavily, 77.9 s a call against 6.78 s.

Two things both runs agree on, and they are the ones to act on. First, the internal pipeline is much slower — 3.5× on synthetic, 11× on real. Second, its transcription has a catastrophic tail: the real run's mean “characters right” is −0.29 against a median of 0.80, meaning a few transcripts are so full of inserted text they are worse than useless, exactly the runaway failure our own Qwen3-ASR showed 16 times. Agreement across two independent evaluations is worth more than either one alone.

Four things that must travel with these numbers.

  • RECONCILED: NO for our own numbers. Not one real production call has been through our pipeline. Sections 2 and 3 are entirely synthetic Thai audio. The real-data run in section 1 is someone else's measurement, read from their published summary and not reproduced here.
  • These are upper bounds. Every label in the corpus is spoken aloud, so the set measures fact extraction under noise, not inferential labelling. Real calls state their outcome far less often; both arms will do worse in production.
  • The ground truth was corrected after a blind audit whose reviewers were models, not people. A human panel is still what would settle those labels.
  • One arm could not be run, so this is a four-arm result, not five.
