2Both of our wins are on benchmarks that cannot separate models

RTR-Fraud — saturated. All 9 model-by-field accuracy cells land between 98.5% and 100%. The worst arm misses 3 items out of 198; the best misses none. Three different model families separated by three items is a benchmark with no room left in it — our +0.00455 is not evidence of an advantage.

Sentiment Telesale — a ground truth that barely varies. Margin +0.00795 on 26 scored for the incumbent, 25 for internal (1 call failed). The parallel project's own code records that 25 of 39 criteria carry a single label across all 26 calls and 5 of 14 sub-categories contain no violation at all, so a constant answer scores 98–99%. Our win is driven by the Compliance dimension — 3 criteria, 2 of which hold one label.

Neither is a reason to doubt the models. Both are reasons not to quote these two wins as evidence for migration, and reasons to fix the benchmarks before the next round.

3Latency — the one result that is unanimous

Our models are slower on every task in the set, by 4.2× to 86.5×. This is the only finding in the workbook that points the same way six times out of six, and it is not marginal: Sentiment QA is 216.2 s a call against 2.5 s, and Tax-Invoice is 79.7 s a page against 2.6 s.

Two of the six rounds also lost a call outright to a stream exceeding 900 s (Retention and Telesale, one each). Whatever the quality verdict turns out to be, throughput is the thing that has to be answered before any of this is deployable.

4What the published headline number actually computes

It is a sum divided by 2, not an average. 'weighted Average F1 Score' is the SUM of the dimension F1 scores divided by 2 in five of the six tabs. With two dimensions that coincides with the mean; with three or four it does not, which is why Sentiment QA publishes 1.2887 and Telesale 1.8704 -- values above 1.0 that cannot be an F1. The divisor is constant within a tab, so no winner changes; the number is not a score and is not comparable across tabs. RTR-Fraud is the exception and carries real weights, recovered exactly as 0.45 / 0.45 / 0.10.

Recall that cannot fail. Recall is exactly 1.0000 in all 8 Tax-Invoice cells and 6 of 9 Sentiment QA cells -- the FN=0 signature. Where it appears, accuracy equals precision and F1 is a monotone transform of it, so four reported metrics are one measurement printed four times. We predicted this from production's code (sentiment_qa/fact_check_task.py:1004-1013); these cells measure it.

Neither point changes who won any task — the divisor is constant within a tab. Both change what the numbers may be quoted as. A headline above 1.0 should not be described as an F1 score, and on Tax-Invoice and Sentiment QA the four reported metrics are not four independent checks.

The printed F1 differs from the harmonic mean of the printed precision and recall by at most 0.02 outside one collapsed Gemma column. That is macro-F1 against micro-averaged precision and recall -- a normal choice, not a defect.

The real-data run is the decision-grade one and it does not favour us. Its ground truth is human-maintained, its audio is real customer calls, and it runs the incumbent on Vertex AI batch. Ours does none of those three.

But read the margin, not the ranking. 37/97 correct against 34/97 -- 3 calls; 61/97 against 58/97 -- 3 calls; 41/153 labels against 33/153 -- 8 labels. With no replicates, three calls out of ninety-seven is not a separation — on outcome and product these two are indistinguishable on this evidence. The reason dimension is the one real gap.

Why the reversal is plausible rather than a contradiction to explain away. Our corpus states every label aloud — a documented property, not a discovery. That removes the need for the inferential step, and inference is exactly what a direct-audio model may be better at, since a transcript throws away prosody, hesitation and tone. So the synthetic set may not merely inflate scores; it may remove the thing the incumbent is good at. That mechanism is a hypothesis. The reversal is a measurement.

8What this says, and what it does not

What the six tasks agree on. Quality is close everywhere — the widest gap in either direction is 0.075. Speed is not close anywhere: we are 4.2× to 86.5× slower, six times out of six. On this evidence the case against migrating is throughput, not accuracy.

What we should not claim. That we beat the incumbent on RTR-Fraud or Telesale — both benchmarks are unable to separate models. That the incumbent beats us on Retention outcome or product — three calls out of ninety-seven, unreplicated, is not a separation. The one clean quality result in the set is Tax-Invoice, where gemini-3.1-pro-preview leads by 0.0201 and Gemma-4-12B collapses outright.

What to fix before the next round. The headline formula, the FN=0 confusion matrices on Tax-Invoice and Sentiment QA, and the Telesale ground truth. All three are cheap, and all three change what the numbers mean rather than what the models do.

Four things that must travel with these numbers.

  • RECONCILED: NO for our own numbers. Not one real production call has been through our pipeline. Sections 6 (synthetic row) and 7 are entirely synthetic Thai audio. Sections 1–5 are the parallel project's measurement, parsed from their published workbook and not reproduced here.
  • Every label in our corpus is spoken aloud, so our synthetic set measures fact extraction under transcription noise, not inferential labelling. Those scores are upper bounds.
  • Our arms disagree with themselves across three identical replicates on 17 to 33 of 138 items. That instability is a standing property of the task, measured in both of our runs. The real-data run has no replicates at all.
  • Our ground truth was corrected after a blind audit whose reviewers were models, not people.
