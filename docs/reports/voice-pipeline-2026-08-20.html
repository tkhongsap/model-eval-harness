Four cells that are deliberately words, not numbers.

"no ASR stage" — Gemini never produces a transcript; audio goes in and JSON comes out of one call. A CER of 0 there would invite a comparison that does not exist.

"internal GPU" — the Token Factory arms have no per-call price. Printing $0.00 would say free where the truth is not metered here.

"not recorded" — Qwen's per-item ASR timings were overwritten when a two-item resume rewrote its run log. The 136 transcripts are intact; only the stopwatch is gone.

">18.4 ASR untimed" — rather than add zero for a stage that really ran, which would report a two-stage pipeline as faster than it is, in the direction that flatters it.

Why every F1 in this table looks low — and why it is the corpus, not the models.

No arm predicted unknown or undefined on this run, and those are 22 of 136 rows, so the highest weighted F1 reachable here is 0.838, not 1.000.

That is a corpus defect, and the distinction decides the fix. The labelling spec (retention_v9_16_body.txt:80) states that a client who “expresses indecision or asks for time to think” is a save. This corpus builds its unknown calls out of exactly those phrases — so a model following its instructions must answer save, and does, on 174 of 186 blocks. The same models emit unknown 33 times and undefined 19 times on the text packs, so they are perfectly willing to use both.

Relabelling only those 15 calls to what the spec says they are — with no prediction changed — lifts every arm by ~0.13: ceiling 0.645 → 0.776, Typhoon 0.600 → 0.725, Qwen 0.588 → 0.720, Gemini 0.495 → 0.607. The ordering does not change, so no verdict flips.

Restricted to the two classes the models actually use, the same runs read Gemini 0.590 · Qwen 0.702 · Typhoon 0.716 — and the ordering is unchanged. The per-class detail shows why: on churn, precision is 0.92–1.00 for all three (when they say churn, they are right) while recall is only 0.47–0.64. Every arm over-predicts save.

That is the real bottleneck, and it is a schema-and-corpus problem rather than a model one. No better transcriber can fix it.

All three columns are scored on the same 136 calls. Typhoon actually transcribed 138; the two extra (ASR-082, ASR-089) are the calls Qwen3-ASR never produced, so no other arm ran them and they are held out of the business figures. Scoring 138 against 136 would compare arms on different item sets. The effect is small — Typhoon’s call_result F1 moves 0.597 → 0.600 — but small is something you learn after restricting, not a reason to skip it.

Two things jump out of the table that the headline numbers alone hide. Gemini's input cost is 3× the internal arms' — 11,466 median input tokens against ~3,700, because the audio itself is tokenised. And Gemini is the most stable arm by a wide margin (6 unstable items against 30 and 37), which is the one operational column where production clearly wins.

A correction to the 19 August headline

The preregistration says every headline figure is computed on replicate 1 alone — "choosing the replicate after seeing three of them is choosing the answer." The scorer was collapsing all three by majority vote. It now defaults to the preregistered rule and stamps the policy into its output. Both policies agree — AHEAD on call_result, indistinguishable elsewhere — so the conclusion survives. It is now verified rather than assumed.

5 · The 30–50k token issue

Correction: we did not reproduce 30–40k output tokens. The number that lands in that range is the input.

Across 120 calls with production’s own prompt: input 30,985–32,825 (median 31,926) on every single call — but output 8,564–17,054 (median 11,961), and zero of 120 reached 30,000.

Three explanations for a 30–40k output were tested and ruled out: an explicit reasoning budget changes nothing (byte-identical result), output does not scale with call length (correlation 0.12), and Gemini 2.5 Pro is lower than Flash. Nothing was truncated.

The input is almost entirely user_config.xlsx — 94,174 characters ≈ 31,400 tokens of Thai field definitions, re-sent in full every call with cached_tokens: 0. If that is the number being read, the lever is context caching or a smaller prompt, not the thinking budget. The drafted ask now requests all three counters so it cannot stay ambiguous.

The thinking measurement below is true either way. Same model, two configurations, measured rather than estimated. The retention task — 1,632 calls already on disk — spends a median of 245 completion tokens with zero reasoning tokens.

Three quarters of the output is thinking, not answer. Turning it off cuts output tokens 71% and still returns the complete 118-key object every time. The three levers that don't work are as informative as the one that does:

  • reasoning: low does nothing — "low" is not low.
  • Retention's decoding (temperature 0, top_p 0) does nothing.
  • Deleting the "think 1…5" prompt block does nothing. Prompt surgery is not the lever; the budget is.

What this cannot show. Production runs on Vertex, which has thinkingBudget; this ran on OpenRouter, which does not. These arms test the prompt and schema faithfully and the budget only by proxy. Nor is accuracy measured — there is no labelled sentiment_qa batch in this repo, so "does capping thinking cost quality" stays open.

One number settles it, costs nothing and contains no customer data: a single real batch response's usageMetadata.thoughtsTokenCount. The ask is drafted in EN and TH.

What to change, in order

  1. thinkingBudget: -1 → a finite value. Three call sites; the highest-volume is the daily batch qa_pipeline_tasks.yml:76, not the fact-check path usually quoted.
  2. The prompt contradicts itself. Lines 18–24 demand five numbered reasoning steps; the Thai line at 26 says the reason must not be step by step. Free to fix.
  3. maxOutputTokens: 65535 is not a backstop — it is the API maximum, so nothing caps a runaway today.

6 · Six defects found and fixed

  1. Typhoon was reported unavailable, and wasn't. The catalog probe used /chat/completions; an ASR model answers /audio/transcriptions. A confident false negative.
  2. The transcriber sent unauthenticated requests — it never loads .env, so a missing key became a bare nginx 401 on every item.
  3. The retry loop discarded the error it retried on, reporting only "failed after N attempts".
  4. The arm→transcript map was duplicated. If the two copies drifted, one arm's calls would be labelled with another arm's transcripts and every number would be internally consistent and wrong.
  5. An unknown --arms value ran zero arms and exited 0.
  6. The ASR test suite could not be collected at all. The documented separate virtualenv did not exist; the full 152-test suite now runs.

7 · Limits, unchanged

  • Synthetic TTS audio, two voices — speaker variety is prosody only.
  • Generator-authored labels. RECONCILED: NO.
  • Every F1 here is an upper bound: the corpus states its labels in ~100% of calls, and the measured leak is published rather than hidden.
  • No arm exceeds ~0.66 on call_result, but not because those calls are unwinnable — an earlier version of this page said so and was wrong. The corpus labels 22 of 136 rows unknown/undefined in a way that contradicts the labelling spec, which routes those same sentences to save. Worth ~0.13 F1 on every arm. The repair is in the corpus.

8 · What comes next

  1. Adopt Typhoon for the ASR stage — on reliability grounds. Expect no business-metric change, and say so up front.
  2. Send the Vertex token ask. One field turns a directional finding into a settled one.
  3. Send Ask 1. Still the only path off RECONCILED: NO.
  4. Fix the corpus, not the labeller. A perfect transcript scores 0.645 because the corpus’s unknown calls are labelled against the spec, not because the labeller is timid. Teaching it to emit unknown on indecision would train it to violate the production spec to score better on bad data. The repair is three edits in asr-eval/scripts/ and costs no model calls.
