† The two shapes are not comparable on one token or latency number. Gemini makes ONE call and its prompt carries the recording as tokens — 9,175 of its 11,615. The pipeline makes TWO: the audio goes to a transcription endpoint that reports no usage at all and bills GPU time, then only text goes to the labeller. So the pipeline's 18.3s is its second call, and its first stage is real work sitting outside every column here.

‡ Qwen ASR could not be scored. The model stopped answering mid-run at 120 of 138 transcripts and has been down since; the labeller it shares with Typhoon stayed healthy, so this is that one model, not the gateway. 120/138 is under the 90% coverage gate, so the arm is reported UNAVAILABLE rather than scored behind a shrunken denominator.

Perfect transcript and Format control are not candidates — they are the ruler. The first shows what the labeller scores when transcription is flawless (0.839), which caps what any ASR improvement can buy. The second contains every formatting difference and zero mishearing, so any gap it does not explain is hearing rather than layout.

Read it this way. On corrected ground truth the candidate beats production on the two dimensions that matter — outcome 0.730 against 0.564, product 0.864 against 0.798 — at zero metered spend and a third of the billed tokens. It is also slower and less repeatable: 18.3s against 5.2s, and 33 answers that changed against 17. And it sits close to its own ceiling (0.730 against 0.839), so the binding constraint is the labelling step, not the audio. This is not yet a migration recommendation: one arm could not be run, the reviewers who corrected the labels were models rather than people, and no production call has been through any of this.
