† Tokens and latency are not one number across these two shapes. Gemini makes one call whose prompt carries the recording as tokens — 9,175 of its 11,615. The pipeline makes two, and its audio goes to an endpoint that reports no usage at all and bills GPU time. The pipeline's end-to-end figure uses the transcription stage measured on the previous run at the same chunk size, because this run's ASR timing record was destroyed by an orphaned process hours after scoring; the label stage, 18.3 s, is this run's.

The candidate is close to its own ceiling. Given a perfect transcript the same labeller reaches only 0.839 on call outcome, against the candidate's 0.730 — so the remaining headroom in the audio stage is about 0.11, and the binding constraint is the labelling step rather than the transcription. A control arm carrying every formatting difference and zero mishearing scores 0.836, which is how we know the gap is hearing rather than layout.

A third arm could not be scored. Qwen3-ASR, the alternative first stage, stopped answering at 120 of 138 transcripts and has been down since; the labeller it shares with Typhoon stayed healthy, so this is that one model rather than the gateway. 120/138 is under the 90% coverage gate, so it is reported UNAVAILABLE rather than scored behind a shrunken denominator.

Read it this way. On corrected ground truth the candidate beats production on both dimensions that matter — outcome 0.730 against 0.564, product 0.864 against 0.798 — at zero metered spend and a third of the billed input tokens. It is also far slower and less repeatable: about 61 s against 5.2 s, and 33 answers that changed across three identical repeats against 17. This is not yet a migration recommendation: one arm could not be run, the reviewers who corrected the labels were models rather than people, and no production call has been through any of this.
