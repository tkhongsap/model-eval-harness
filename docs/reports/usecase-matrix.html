Accuracy is not independent information on most tasks. RTR-Fraud, Tax-Invoice and Sentiment Telesale each score it identical to Precision or Recall on every dimension -- a consequence of those tabs' confusion matrices being built with FN=0 or TN=0, the same construction already documented for this workbook. Only Sentiment MNP, Sentiment Retention and one dimension of Sentiment QA carry an accuracy that could not already be read off a column shown elsewhere. It is published here because it is a real number the workbook prints, not because it corroborates the F1 column with a second, independent look.

  • RTR-Fraud — identical to Precision and Recall on every dimension
  • Tax-Invoice — identical to Precision on every dimension
  • Sentiment QA — 1 of 3 dimensions independent (What the call was about - here all four differ); the rest equal Precision or Recall
  • Sentiment MNP — an independent measurement, not derivable from Precision or Recall
  • Sentiment Telesale — identical to Recall on every dimension
  • Sentiment Retention — an independent measurement, not derivable from Precision or Recall

The gap is concentrated in one stage, and it is not the labelling model. On the 2 tasks where the internal model reads the source directly — RTR-Fraud and Tax-Invoice — it reaches 99.2% of production. On the 4 that put a speech-to-text stage in front of it, 96.4%. The workbook corroborates the mechanism from the inside: the internal transcript on Retention scores a mean character error rate above 1.0, and Sentiment QA's transcript quality tracks which speech model ran rather than which prompt.

Two cautions. The two direct tasks are also the two where the incumbent scores 0.9877 and 0.9993, so part of that closeness is a ceiling with little headroom to lose, and it is only two data points. And neither source publishes a single token, second or dollar for the speech stage, so this identifies where the gap is without establishing what closing it would cost.

The four questions this page has to survive.

  • “97% of an F1 is not 97% as good.” Correct. On an error basis the same six results read as 1.296× the errors (0.0812 against 0.1052 mean 1−F1). Both figures are true and both belong in the room.
  • “You picked the best model per task.” Partly — and the exposure is small. On 3 of the six tasks only one internal arm ran, so nothing was selected. On two more the candidates are the same model under different prompts. Only RTR-Fraud, margin 0.000855 over Gemma-4-12B. Against the mean of every internal arm (0.8764) this selection flatters by 0.0184.
  • “How much slower?” 4.2× to 86.5× per item. Per-item latency on the 5 tasks that publish it for the selected arm. Sentiment QA does not: its cell reads 'cannot meansure' and the round was 'resumed over several days', so that run cannot be timed. The absence flatters the internal side, so the figure that does exist for that task is published beside it -- its other configuration ran at 86.5x, the slowest of the whole set.
  • “What do we save?” This evaluation cannot say what migrating would save. Three inputs are missing: production volume per task, the internal endpoint's cost per hour and its throughput, and the cost of the speech-to-text stage. Asking for those three is a firmer position than a saving that cannot be evidenced.

Two tasks are shown as parity rather than as wins (RTR-Fraud, Sentiment Telesale). Both leads are inside their own measurement noise. RTR-Fraud's +0.0045 was bought with 594 internal model calls against the incumbent's 198 over the same 198 submissions. Telesale's +0.0080 is over 25 calls scored against 26, and turns on one dimension of four -- the incumbent leads the other three, and the one internal wins carries 3 criteria against the others' 15, 12 and 9. Present these as parity, not as wins.

Sentiment Retention is the widest gap at 91.5% and it stays in the headline. Excluding it would read 98.5%. Sentiment Retention was rescored between two workbook revisions this week -- outcome F1 moved 0.4537 to 0.9147 on identical calls, tokens and latency -- which makes it arguably provisional. It is NOT excluded from the headline, because the rescore moved both arms: the incumbent led before it (93.4%) and leads by more after it (91.5%). Dropping the task improves the figure by 1.1 points without a reason that survives the question 'did it change who leads?'

Cost. The incumbent's side of this evaluation cost $28.57 at list rates. Not metered. Neither source publishes a cost, price, GPU-hour or utilisation figure for the internal endpoint in any of its six tabs. That is a measurement never taken -- it is not $0, not free and not n/a.

What this page must not be read as saying.

  • That the internal models match the incumbent, or that the gap is within noise. They are behind under every defensible aggregation, and neither source publishes any dispersion or interval that could support a noise claim.
  • That the internal side uses fewer tokens, or that this is efficiency. It is an artefact of an unmeasured speech stage and a different tokeniser.
  • That migrating saves money. No internal cost figure exists in either source.
  • That 'weighted Average F1' is a weighted average. Only RTR-Fraud is; Tax-Invoice covers 2 of its 4 dimensions and the rest are plain means.
  • That Accuracy is an independent check on the F1 column. On RTR-Fraud, Tax-Invoice and Sentiment Telesale it is arithmetically identical to Precision or Recall; only MNP, Retention and one dimension of QA carry a genuinely independent figure.

What the incumbent's runs cost: $28.57 across all 6 of them. Each is input × input_rate + (output + thinking) × output_rate, at OpenRouter standard-tier rates read from openrouter.ai/api/v1/models on 2026-08-24:

  • google/gemini-2.5-flash — $0.30 per 1M text input, $1.00 per 1M audio input, $2.50 per 1M output (standard tier)
  • google/gemini-3.1-pro-preview — $2.00 per 1M text input, $12.00 per 1M output (standard tier)

These runs went through Vertex AI batch, not OpenRouter, so this answers what they would cost on OpenRouter at the standard tier rather than what was invoiced. OpenRouter also carries :batch slugs at roughly half, which would put the same six runs near $14.28; its own documentation hedges batch as “typically billed at 50%” and routes it through a separate endpoint, so the standard tier is the figure quoted here.

What each run cost in total, since the table now shows only the per-item average: RTR-Fraud $0.25 over 198 submissions, Tax-Invoice $12.85 over 147 pages, Sentiment QA $9.59 over 180 calls, Sentiment MNP $2.53 over 100 calls, Sentiment Telesale $1.36 over 26 calls, Sentiment Retention $1.98 over 97 calls.

†The 4 audio use cases are priced at the audio input rate, which OpenRouter charges at $1.00 per 1M against $0.30 for text — 3.3×. Those runs send the call recording straight into the model, so the audio is what their input total is mostly made of. The system prompt inside that total is text and cheaper, and the workbook publishes one input figure that cannot be split — so those four per-item figures are upper bounds. Pricing every input token as text instead gives QA $0.0230, MNP $0.0147, Telesale $0.0216, Retention $0.0094 per call — roughly half. The truth sits between, nearer the printed figure.

Thinking tokens are inside the billable output, not the printed one. On Sentiment MNP the incumbent prints 176,626 output tokens and 227,974 thinking tokens separately; costing the printed line alone would understate that run's billable output by 129%. OpenRouter's internal_reasoning rate equals its completion rate on both models here, so those tokens cost exactly what ordinary output costs.

This is a rate-card estimate, not a bill. OpenRouter discounts cached input heavily — $0.03 per 1M against $0.30 on 2.5 Flash — and cached tokens sit inside the reported input total where a run aggregate cannot separate them, so the input side is an upper bound on that count too. Tax invoice extraction is priced on the cheaper ≤200K-token band, which its mean of 18,756 tokens per request supports — that is a mean, so a few long requests could still have repriced at double. That override is in OpenRouter's JSON only; its model page does not show it.

*RTR-Fraud rests on an assumption. That tab names no model for its incumbent column — it has a runtime row but no model row, unlike every other tab. It is costed as google/gemini-2.5-flash because that is the model named in four of the five other tabs. If it actually ran a Pro-tier model the same tokens cost 6.2× more — $0.0077 per submission rather than $0.0012, and $1.53 for the run rather than $0.25.

Three things that would mislead if read straight off this table.

  • Token counts are not comparable between the two sides. The incumbent's input includes the audio itself, charged as tokens; ours is a transcript, so its input is text for a second model. Fewer tokens on our side is a different pipeline shape, not efficiency.
  • “Not metered” is not free. Self-hosted runs produce no invoice, but the GPU hours behind them are real and are not in this table.
  • The weighted average F1 is comparable within a column pair, not across use cases. Each use case scores different dimensions against different label spaces, and several of these benchmarks cannot separate the arms at all.
  • Neither is the cost row, and for a blunter reason: the units differ. RTR-Fraud's figure is per submission — one RTR_Code folder holding up to three photos, so it is not a cost per image and no per-image figure can be derived, because the corpus never publishes how many photos each folder holds. Tax-Invoice's is per page, and its 147 pages sit inside 32 files, so a cost per document is 4.6× higher. The four sentiment figures are per call.
