Two stages or one

Production sends the audio to Gemini and gets labels back in a single call. Our pipeline transcribes first, then labels the text — two models, two hops. Here is what each step costs and what it buys.

138 synthetic Thai retention calls  ·  3 replicates  ·  2,070 label calls, zero failures  ·  α = 1/64  ·  RECONCILED: NO

1 · The two shapes, and where the time goes

The internal pipeline reads the call better, and the margin is solid. On call_result — the save/churn decision the business acts on — it is AHEAD of production at α = 1/64, clearing the decision band by +10 and surviving 20,000 bootstrap resamples 98.3% of the time.

It is also 8–10× slower per call, because a transcription step now exists that did not before. For batch labelling that is irrelevant; for anything interactive it is the whole story.

4 · What this means for the ASR choice

Typhoon is clearly the better transcriber and it does not change the business answer. It halves the character error rate and removes the runaway failure mode entirely — 16 catastrophic transcripts to zero — yet Typhoon and Qwen are indistinguishable end to end on all three dimensions.

The reason is that stage 2 is now the constraint. A labeller reading a perfect transcript scores 0.787; Typhoon's pipeline already scores 0.711. There is only 0.076 of headroom left upstream, and Typhoon has taken most of what was available.

So adopt Typhoon on reliability grounds — one call in eight no longer produces garbage — and do not expect the business numbers to move when you do. Note also that Qwen is 1.7× faster at transcription (25.0 s vs 43.2 s), which is the one column where it wins.

5 · Limits worth stating

  • Synthetic TTS audio, two Thai voices — speaker variety is prosody only.
  • Labels authored by the generator, not by a human. RECONCILED: NO: no production call, no production label and no human adjudication has been through any part of this. It is a screening eval, not a production gate.
  • Scores are upper bounds. The corpus states its labels in nearly every call; real calls do so far less often, so all three arms will do worse in production.
  • 8 of 138 calls are unwinnable for every arm — a known corpus limitation, capping the reachable F1 at 0.942 rather than 1.000. Accepted, not a defect in any model.
  • Cost is only half-measured. Gemini bills per call and the figure is real; the internal GPU has no per-call price, which is not the same as being free. A like-for-like comparison needs the amortised GPU cost, which is outside this harness.
