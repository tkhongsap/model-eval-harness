**`manners`**
* predefined categories:

* Meet:
  No inappropriate service behavior by the agent toward the customer is found throughout the call, or only events that are not manners issues are found, e.g.: the customer is unhappy about system/policy limitations; the customer accuses the agent of interrupting or arguing but there is no clear evidence; a flat/indifferent tone; the customer getting annoyed at repeated explanations; unintentional simultaneous talking after which the agent stops to let the customer continue; conversations with internal staff/technicians/Shop that are not addressed to the customer directly; humming, other people's voices, or ambient sound that is not the Agent speaking to the customer.

* Below:
  Clear evidence that the agent behaved inappropriately toward the customer, e.g.: rude words, aggression, sarcasm, mocking, arguing; a hard/pressuring tone or abnormal word stress; repeatedly interrupting or cutting the customer off while they are still explaining, without letting them continue; or inappropriate phrasing such as telling the customer to release the line / not taking the call, or criticizing the customer to another person while still involved with the customer's case.

* Advanced voice analytics rules:

1. Use Speaker Diarization to separate Agent / Customer / Other speaker / Background before judging. Never use other people's voices, team voices, interjections, humming, or environmental sounds to judge the Agent's manners.
2. Use Overlap & Turn-taking Analysis to check interruptions: only when the Agent starts speaking while the Customer is still speaking continuously, and the Agent does not stop / yield the turn back to the customer, does it count as Below.
3. Short cross-talk, speaking simultaneously because the agent believed the customer had finished, or the Agent stopping immediately when the customer continues, counts as Meet.
4. Use Prosody Analysis to detect a hard tone from pitch, intensity, stress, speaking rate, and abnormal emphasis. There must be evidence combined with inappropriate wording/context for it to count as Below.
5. A flat, indifferent, sluggish, or unenthusiastic tone alone is not a manners Below — consider it under enthusiasm instead.
6. Explaining repeatedly until the customer is confused/annoyed is not directly a manners issue — consider it under communication_skill, unless accompanied by a clearly hard tone, arguing, sarcasm, or clear cutting-off.
7. The customer blaming, complaining, or feeling the agent is not solving the problem is not sufficient to judge Below; there must be real evidence of impolite behavior from the Agent.
8. During Hold, After-hold, Internal consult, or talks with internal staff, assess manners only when that speech directly affects the customer or is clearly inappropriate talk about the customer.
9. Before judging Below, you must identify at least 1 piece of evidence from: inappropriate wording, a hard tone with context, repeated interruption without stopping, arguing/sarcasm/blaming the customer.
10. If the evidence is unclear, or is only an inference from the customer's emotions, judge Meet.
11. If agents are found talking among themselves during a call transfer, do not assess it under manners — assess it under transfer_standard only.

**`enthusiasm`**

Enthusiasm evaluation rules:
- Use supporting voice analysis such as tone, pitch, energy, pace, volume, silence, response latency, and emotional warmth.

- Give Below only when there is clear evidence of at least 2 of:

      3. Not responding to the customer's emotions, concerns, or dissatisfaction.

      5. A clear lack of words or behavior showing readiness to help.

Decision rule:
- If only a "flat/formal tone" is found, but the agent is still polite, answers completely, and helps appropriately with no indifferent behavior, judge Meet.
- If the audio evidence is unclear, base the judgment on the service behavior, and do not judge Below from tone alone.
- If agents are found talking among themselves during a call transfer, do not assess it under enthusiasm — assess it under transfer_standard only.

**`communication_skill`**
**communication_skill Evaluation Logic**

**Meet**
The agent communicates clearly, understandably, and to the point; explains key information / steps / technical terms so the customer can understand; the speaking rhythm does not impair the customer's understanding.

**Below**
Give Below only when there is clear evidence that "the agent's communication in the current call" is the main cause of the customer finding it hard to understand, being confused, failing to grasp the point, or asking again — e.g. rambling, skipping steps, disordered information, using technical terms without explanation, speaking too fast / too slow / stumbling / talking over the customer in a way that genuinely impairs understanding.

**N/A**
No agent audio; only the customer's voice; system / signal failure; or insufficient evidence for a fair assessment.
---
## Decision Rules

1. Never give Below merely because the customer asks again, argues, does not listen, does not accept the process, negotiates, or is unhappy with the outcome. If the agent explained clearly, correctly, and with reasons, judge Meet.
2. If the customer is confused by their own information — e.g. misremembering, misunderstanding conditions, expecting more than the process allows, or wanting something outside the conditions — but the agent explained clearly, judge Meet.
3. If the customer asks again, separate the cause:
* because the agent explained unclearly / gave conflicting information / rambled / skipped steps = Below
* because the customer would not accept / negotiated / would not listen / the process is complex but the agent explained clearly = Meet

4. Never use problems belonging to other criteria to judge communication_skill — e.g. incomplete probing, failing to analyze the problem, failing to resolve the issue, failing to state the SLA, process limitations, tone / enthusiasm / service mind — unless the communication genuinely misled the customer.
5. Never score the behavior of a previous agent against the agent in the current call.
6. Use Voice Analytics as supporting evidence only — e.g. speech rate, pause, fluency, overlap, clarity, and customer reaction — and give Below only when there is both "an abnormal audio signal" and "a real impact on the customer's understanding".
7. If agents are found talking among themselves during a call transfer, do not assess it under communication_skill — assess it under transfer_standard only.

---

## Final Rule
* **Below** = the agent communicated so unclearly that the customer genuinely did not understand.
* **Meet** = the agent communicated clearly, but the customer did not listen / accept / negotiated / was unhappy with the outcome.
* **N/A** = no audio, or insufficient evidence.
